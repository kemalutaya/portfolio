I'm Kem Alutaya, based in the Philippines, working remotely for teams across the U.S. and UK. My annotation and transcription work has been with Innodata and TELUS Digital AI, both part-time, across roughly eight months in 2025 and 2026.

I annotate and transcribe to a client's specification. I don't set the labelling schema or make research decisions about what the data should mean.

Quality

How I keep the work clean.

Annotation quality is mostly habits. These are mine.

Throughput

Above quota by design

Innodata set per-task KPI quotas with real consequences for missing them. Rather than pacing to the number, I ran above it so a complex batch or a bad day never became a missed target.

Guidelines

Read first, label second

I go through the annotation guideline properly before the first item, including the edge-case rules most people skim. Re-reading it mid-batch when something feels off has saved more rework than working faster ever would.

Ambiguity

Flag it, don't guess it

When two labels both defend and the guideline doesn't settle it, I raise it rather than picking one quietly. One flagged question usually resolves a pattern across the whole batch.

Self-review

I check before QA does

I validate my own output against the guideline before submitting. Catching my own drift is cheaper for everyone than having a reviewer catch it downstream.

Consistency

Same call on item 400 as item 4

Long batches invite drift. I keep my own notes on how I've been resolving recurring edge cases so a decision made early holds all the way through.

Confidentiality

Used to sensitive data

Private workspace, encrypted and password-protected devices, two-factor authentication. Five years working under HIPAA rules with patient records, and I'll sign whatever NDA or confidentiality terms a project requires.

Process Improvement

I automate my own repetitive work.

Case Study Highest Impact

Cutting a Three-Hour Daily Task to One

3 hrs → 1 hr Daily preparation task, before and afterEstimated, based on the previous manual process.
Problem
Three recurring daily tasks were fully manual, highly repetitive, and error-prone in predictable ways: wrong recipient, wrong date, wrong attachment, typos. Exactly the failure profile of any high-repetition task, annotation included.
What I did
Mapped the manual process, identified which fields were genuinely repeatable and which needed a human decision, and built the repeatable part into Excel, Outlook, and Word macros. I used only tools already permitted on a locked-down remote desktop, because installing anything new required IT approval.
The judgment call
I automated the mechanical part and deliberately kept a human review step before anything went out. The goal was never to remove myself from the loop, it was to spend my attention on the part that actually needed it.
Result
The task dropped from about three hours a day to about one. The workflow was presented and approved for operational use.
